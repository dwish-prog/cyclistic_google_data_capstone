# Cyclistic Bike-Share Case Study: Commuter Hubs vs. Leisure Cycles

An end-to-end data analytics project exploring how annual members and casual riders use fictional company 'Cyclistic' bike-shares differently in Chicago. This analysis processes 5.9 million historical trips to deliver data-backed marketing strategies aimed at converting high-value casual riders into long-term annual subscribers.

## 🛠️ Tech Stack & Skills

- **Data Engineering & Querying:** SQL (CTEs, Window Functions, Aggregate Joins)
- **Data Analysis & Formatting:** Excel (Pivot Tables, Hierarchical Multi-Level Timelines)
- **Data Visualization & BI:** Tableau (Spatial Heatmaps, Density Mark Mapping)
- **Version Control:** Git / GitHub

## 📈 Key Insights

1. **The Commuter vs. Leisure Divide:** Annual members exhibit rigid, bi-modal weekday traffic spikes precisely during rush hours (07:00–09:00 and 16:00–18:00) with highly inelastic, short trip durations (10–13 minutes). Conversely, casual riders dominate weekend traffic, showing smooth afternoon leisure curves and highly volatile, longer trip durations.
2. **Geographic Footprints:** Aggregated station coordinate mapping reveals distinct spatial clustering. Annual members heavily utilize inland, commercial, and transit-connected downtown hubs during the week. Casual riders cluster intensely around waterfront recreational zones, parks, and tourist landmarks, particularly during summer surges.

## 💡 Strategic Recommendations

- **Targeted Seasonal Conversions:** Launch high-impact marketing campaigns in early spring tailored specifically around recreational waterfront hubs where casual ridership heavily clusters.
- **Value-Frame the Annual Tier:** Construct promotional messaging directed at frequent weekend and summer users that explicitly calculates and illustrates the long-term cost savings of converting from a per-ride pass to an annual membership.
- **Digital Engagement Optimization:** Utilize app-based push notifications near high-volume weekend leisure stations to capture casual riders during peak activity periods.
